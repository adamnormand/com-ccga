Volunteer

Teamwork is one of our greatest strengths, and there are many ways to volunteer — on and off the water.

Joining a SAR crew

  • Be the age of majority under provincial legislation
  • Be in good physical condition
  • Complete the Pleasure Craft Operator Certificate course
  • Hold Marine Basic First Aid or equivalent, with CPR/AED Level C

Each region has additional requirements — for example, vessel ownership in Newfoundland and Labrador, or a fitness test and police check in British Columbia. Contact the auxiliary nearest you.

More ways to help

  • Advocate for boating safety
  • Vessel maintenance and equipment management
  • Fundraising and special events
  • Media and marketing

Volunteer spotlight: Brian McShane

A Central & Arctic volunteer for more than 16 years, Brian is Arctic Operations Manager in Yellowknife, NWT, supporting 20 auxiliary units and more than 239 volunteers. He received the 2024 Mustang Survival Rescue of the Year at the Canadian Safe Boating Awards: on August 17, 2024, after a 16-foot vessel capsized on Great Slave Lake, he rescued seven people — four adults, two children and an eight-week-old infant.

Training

Our volunteers are mission ready: instructor-led operational readiness training, plus joint exercises with other SAR agencies.

Teamwork

Many challenging, rewarding roles within the camaraderie of your crew.

Response time

Our average response time is 25 minutes to deploy resources to a marine emergency.
