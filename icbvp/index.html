Indigenous Community Boat Volunteer Program

Indigenous and coastal communities have a long history of responding to marine incidents, and are often the first — and only — responders able to reach remote locations in time to make a difference.

The Indigenous Community Boat Volunteer Program (ICBVP), funded by the Canadian Coast Guard, supports Indigenous communities to purchase a boat or the equipment they need to take part in the marine safety response system as active CCGA members. Once the program is established in a community, members are supported by their regional auxiliary. Launched in 2017, the program continues with renewed funding under the Oceans Protection Plan.

Goals

  • Increase Indigenous participation in Canada's marine response system
  • Provide equipment so communities can join the Auxiliary, where they receive marine emergency response training and ongoing support

What funding can cover

  • Purchase, retrofit or repair of a SAR-capable boat
  • Equipment, including Transport Canada required safety equipment
  • A boat shelter and other related equipment

The program does not prescribe the type of boat, since each community's needs and environment are unique — but all boats and equipment must meet Transport Canada and CCGA requirements. Funding is provided 100% up front.

Eligibility

First Nations, Inuit and Métis bands and settlements; Indigenous governments under self-government or modern treaties; Indigenous communities or groups; district, chiefs' and tribal councils; Indigenous-led organizations or those serving an Indigenous population; and corporations whose major shareholder is Indigenous.

A CCGA letter of support is mandatory. Non-Indigenous organizations serving an Indigenous population also need a letter from a band council, hamlet office or community council.

Applying

Applications are accepted once a year, typically due in the fall for funding starting the following April 1. They are assessed by a committee of SAR professionals, and funding flows through a contribution agreement between the Canadian Coast Guard and the community. Your regional auxiliary can provide the form and help with the application.

Before you apply

  • Community capacity — are there already Auxiliary members in the community?
  • Storage — is there a safe place for the boat and equipment? If not, a shelter can be included.
  • Maintenance — can the community maintain the boat and equipment?
  • Authority — the person applying needs financial and signing authority.

Receiving funding is a commitment to remain an active Auxiliary member and to use the assets for search and rescue for the initial life cycle of the boat or equipment, or 10 years, whichever is less.
