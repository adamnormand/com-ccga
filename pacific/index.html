Who we are

A key part of the marine search and rescue system

Royal Canadian Marine Search and Rescue (RCMSAR) is the Canadian Coast Guard Auxiliary for the Pacific region: a not-for-profit, volunteer-based organization operating rescue stations from Vancouver's busy harbour to the remote waters of the North Coast. Its area of operations covers more than 25,000 km of rugged coastline known for challenging weather and sea conditions.

Volunteers have been part of marine rescue on the BC coast for more than 100 years. In 1978, volunteer groups across Canada created the Canadian Coast Guard Auxiliary; in 2012 the Pacific auxiliary took the name Royal Canadian Marine Search and Rescue. The mission remains the same — saving lives on the water.

How we save lives

Through an agreement with the Canadian Coast Guard, crews are tasked to marine emergencies by the Joint Rescue Coordination Centre in Victoria. RCMSAR is also available for humanitarian aid on the water when called upon by provincial authorities. Each station is run by station leaders and a local governance board, with oversight and support from headquarters in Sooke. RCMSAR crews handle roughly one quarter of all marine rescues in the region each year.

Vision and values

Excellence in community-based marine safety, built on safety, trust and teamwork — and a “one-crew” culture.
