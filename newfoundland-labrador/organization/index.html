Organization

Structure

CCGA (NL) Inc. is controlled by its Board of Directors and jointly administered by its Executive, employees and the CCGA Regional Coordinator. The regional presidents form the CCGA National Council, which negotiates the contribution agreement with the Minister of Fisheries and Oceans — usually for five years, and valid only once signed by every president.

Operations

Operationally, CCGA (NL) is divided into ten districts and sixty-three sectors, so the SAR controller can quickly pinpoint available resources. An annual membership renewal keeps information at the Maritime Rescue Sub-Centre (MRSC) accurate, and members call in changes affecting their vessels so updates reach the MRSC daily.

Members have responded to major incidents such as the Marie Teixeira Vilarinho (September 1980), Norma & Gladys and Taskall (October 1984) and Zidani (November 1986), and have received awards including the Commissioner's Commendation, Medals of Bravery and awards from the Canadian Power and Sail Squadrons.

Membership responsibilities

Members:

  • Make available seaworthy, crewed vessels meeting CCGA and CCG standards, with inspections at enrolment and after — or contribute a skilled volunteer effort instead
  • Take the training the CCG and CCGA require
  • Follow CCGA bylaws and guidelines and conduct authorized activities prudently and safely

Coast Guard, through the Auxiliary:

  • Reimburses out-of-pocket expenses for authorized activities
  • Provides insurance for members and vessels during authorized activities
  • Keeps members informed and helps them build skills
